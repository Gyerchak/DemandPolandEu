Trade & Tax Guide

Running a cross-border trading business involves a few core rules. Always confirm current numbers with a tax advisor — this is a reminder, not legal advice.

  • Cost to get goods to you = purchase price + freight + import duty + handling. That's what you pay until it ships to you.
  • VAT on sale: when you sell, you charge VAT at your category/market rate (e.g. 23% standard, reduced for some goods). You owe it on the sale price.
  • Home market = Poland (VAT 23% standard). Visegrad is your main export/import market.
  • Duty: within the EU single market duty is 0%. Buying outside the EU (China, Turkiye, UK...) adds import duty.
  • Register for VAT (and possibly Intrastat + an EORI number for EU trade) once you trade above thresholds.
  • Keep invoices for every purchase and sale — you need them for VAT deduction and records.
  • Invoice in EUR (default currency here) and watch FX for non-EUR markets (China CNY, Turkiye TRY, etc).

Cost: cost = buy + freight + duty + handling (payments until it ships to you).

Profit: profit = sell − (cost + VAT), VAT applied on the sale price when enabled.

Opportunity score: 100 × success_rate × √(margin / margin_ref) — high score needs BOTH good sell probability and a worthwhile margin.
